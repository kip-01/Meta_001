About

I am a project and resilience practitioner based in Kakamega. Since 2016 I have worked wherever climate, livelihoods and conflict overlap: fish farms at Sagana, savings groups and special schools in the east, pastoral Samburu, and a mineral plant in Ikolomani.

My habit is to start with the people who will be affected and the records that already exist, then design the smallest plan that can actually be delivered. Most of what is on this site began that way.

Today I run operations at TerraHeal Pharmaceuticals and write proposals and articles on the side.

How I work

Listen and map

Who is affected, who decides, who has been left out. I start with people and records, not assumptions.

Frame with evidence

Use local data and the community's own analysis to define one problem worth solving.

Design the smallest thing that works

Cut scope until time and money match the ambition, as with the 13-school nursery project.

Build with the people affected

Farmers, elders, teachers, youth groups and county staff share the work, and the credit.

Measure, then adjust

Short feedback loops, field dashboards, honest reports, and changes when the data says so.

Hand over

Groups, hubs and county teams that can carry on after the project steps back.
